# Trainer Manual – I CAN Pilot

## Facilitator role
Create a safe, respectful, structured environment. Model skills, support practice without taking over, include quieter children, adapt tasks, document objective evidence, and route concerns through the safeguarding lead. Use specialist support where an activity exceeds facilitator competence.

## Before each session
1. Review objectives, steps, and age suitability.
2. Check participant list, authorized pickup arrangements, and relevant accommodations.
3. Inspect venue, exits, toilets, furniture, and materials.
4. Remove unapproved tools, choking hazards, damaged equipment, and trip hazards.
5. Prepare visual instructions and accessible alternatives.
6. Confirm safeguarding contact, first-aid arrangement, and emergency procedure.
7. Prepare attendance and incident records.

## Delivery
Welcome/check-in; introduce challenge; demonstrate skill; guided practice; individual/team application; reflection/evidence; cleanup and verified handover. Adapt pacing. Never force disclosure or public performance.

## Facilitation prompts
“What makes you think that?” “What else could work?” “How might we test it?” “What changed after your test?” Give specific feedback about strategy and evidence. Avoid shaming, sarcasm, ranking, fixed labels, and comparisons.

## Differentiation
For children needing support: one-step prompts, visual schedules, examples, extra processing time, manageable choices. For children ready for extension: add constraints, compare alternatives, test assumptions, or explain trade-offs. Do not make one child responsible for another.

## Evidence-based assessment
Record what the child did, what support was needed, context, artifact/explanation, and next step.
- 1: Needs continuous support.
- 2: Performs with guidance.
- 3: Independent in a familiar context.
- 4: Transfers the skill to a new context.
- 5: Improves a solution and explains why.
This is not an IQ score, diagnosis, or permanent label.

## Post-session record
Log attendance and handover; activities and timing changes; evidence; participation observations; safety issues/near misses; next-session adjustments; and parent communication through approved channels.

## Staff readiness
Use role-appropriate screening, references and qualification checks as lawful. Prepare staff in safeguarding, boundaries, positive behavior support, emergency evacuation, first aid appropriate to duties, privacy, and activity safety. Include interview and practical demonstration.

## Safety gates
Do not run an activity unless age-appropriate, risk-assessed, adequately supervised, and supplied with inspected materials, a stop/help signal, and safe alternative. Pilot exclusions: live fire, dangerous tools, mains electricity, hot equipment, hazardous chemicals, unsupervised internet/AI use.

## Parent communication
Share factual observations, evidence, and realistic next steps. Never promise guaranteed academic, psychological, technical, or career outcomes.
