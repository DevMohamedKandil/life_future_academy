# LIFE360 Method – v2.1 Integrated Edition

**Status:** Approved strategic direction | **Pilot audience:** ages 9–12  
**Positioning:** An integrated development method helping children discover potential, practice useful capabilities and demonstrate progress through evidence.

## 1. Design principles
1. Integrated, not fragmented: each experience connects multiple dimensions.
2. Learn by doing: challenges and authentic artifacts anchor learning.
3. Child-centered progression: compare each child with their own baseline, never public ranking.
4. Literacy everywhere: reading, writing, listening and speaking are embedded throughout.
5. Talent is explored, not assigned: repeated exposure replaces one-off labels.
6. Family partnership: parents receive evidence and practical home support.
7. Safe by design: safeguarding, privacy, accessibility and age suitability are release gates.
8. Evidence before scale: expand only after learning, demand, safety and economics are validated.

## 2. Eight dimensions and integrated extensions
| Dimension | Core capability | Extensions woven in |
|---|---|---|
| Life Skills | Independence, daily responsibility, financial awareness | Science/environment, budgeting, challenges |
| Self Management | Time, habits, priorities, emotional self-awareness | Self-leadership, reflection, parent-supported practice |
| Crisis Readiness | Prevention, risk awareness, help-seeking | First Aid & Safety awareness |
| Maker Skills | Design, build, test, improve | Science Lab, maker challenges, expo artifacts |
| Thinking Skills | Logic, evidence, problem-solving, decisions | Leadership, entrepreneurship, challenge design |
| Future Skills | Digital literacy, computational thinking, AI awareness | Digital Citizenship, privacy, verification |
| Talent Discovery | Explore interests and strengths over time | Career Discovery, clubs, portfolio evidence |
| Literacy & Communication | Reading, writing, listening, presentation | Teamwork, negotiation, storytelling, expo |

Approved extensions: Challenges, Portfolio, Leadership, Digital Citizenship, First Aid, Science, Career Discovery, Parent Academy, Future Expo and Digital Platform. They are delivery systems and experiences, not ten extra isolated subjects.

## 3. Learning cycle
**LEARN → PRACTICE → SOLVE → CREATE → REFLECT → SHARE**
Learn a concept; practice with support; solve a meaningful problem; create an artifact; reflect on choices and improvement; share approved evidence.

## 4. Challenge architecture
Each challenge specifies age band, scenario, problem, target dimensions, success criteria, constraints, roles, prompts, safety assessment, literacy task, deliverable, rubric, reflection and optional home extension. Multiple solutions are welcome; never reward speed or public competition over inclusion and safety.

## 5. Achievement Passport
A cumulative record of baseline goals, challenge participation, artifacts, skill evidence, reading/writing samples, child reflections, talent/career exploration, facilitator feedback and next steps. Photos/audio/video are optional and require separate informed permission. Minimize data and define access, retention and deletion.

## 6. Leadership and Digital Citizenship
Rotate optional roles such as facilitator, materials steward, timekeeper, researcher, builder, reporter and presenter. Practice listening, respectful disagreement, decision-making and shared responsibility. Digital Citizenship covers privacy, safe communication, cyberbullying response, digital footprints, misinformation, attribution and AI limitations. Use supervised tools and synthetic data.

## 7. First Aid, Science and Safety
Children learn prevention, recognizing when help is needed, alerting a trusted adult and not placing themselves at risk. Hands-on first-aid instruction requires a suitably qualified instructor and approved age-appropriate content; children do not replace responders. Science activities must be risk-assessed and supervised. Exclude live fire, hazardous chemicals, sharp power tools, hot equipment, mains electricity and unsafe biological materials.

## 8. Career Discovery and Parent Academy
Career Discovery offers broad rotating exposure to science, engineering, health, design, arts, business, agriculture, public service, communication and technology. It is not an aptitude test or career prediction. Parent Academy supports independence, reading at home, constructive response to mistakes, safe digital habits and interest exploration without pressure.

## 9. Assessment rubric
| Level | Observable description |
|---|---|
| 1 – Needs support | Needs continuous guidance |
| 2 – Guided | Performs with prompts or partial assistance |
| 3 – Independent | Performs independently in a familiar context |
| 4 – Transfer | Applies skill in a changed context |
| 5 – Innovate & explain | Improves an approach and explains reasoning |

Assess behavior and artifacts, not personality or innate ability. Provide accommodations and multiple ways to demonstrate learning. No public leaderboard.

## 10. Activity quality standard
Every activity specifies objective, dimensions, age suitability, materials, preparation, facilitator steps, adaptations, safety controls, literacy component, evidence, reflection, parent/home link and data captured.

## 11. Safeguarding and scope
LIFE360 is an internal academy framework, not accreditation. Confirm Egyptian legal requirements before operation. Collect minimum necessary data, define lawful basis and retention, restrict access, and do not upload identifiable child data to third-party AI services without appropriate lawful basis, safeguards and required permissions.
