# LIFE360 Method – Framework v2.0

## Purpose

LIFE360 is the academy's integrated learning framework for helping children aged 9–12 build practical capabilities, discover interests, communicate clearly, and approach unfamiliar challenges with confidence and responsibility.

It is an internal educational framework, not a government or international accreditation.

## The eight dimensions

### 1. Life Skills
**Focus:** Practical independence and everyday responsibility.

Examples:
- Organizing personal belongings and a learning space.
- Following safe instructions.
- Understanding needs versus wants.
- Basic shopping and household planning simulations.
- Taking responsibility for age-appropriate tasks.

Evidence:
- Completes a task safely.
- Explains the steps.
- Identifies when adult assistance is needed.

### 2. Self Management
**Focus:** Time, priorities, habits, and self-regulation.

Examples:
- Daily and weekly planning.
- Breaking a task into steps.
- Prioritization.
- Reflecting on distractions and progress.

Evidence:
- Creates a realistic plan.
- Follows and revises the plan.
- Explains why a priority changed.

### 3. Crisis Readiness
**Focus:** Awareness, prevention, and safe help-seeking.

Examples:
- Recognizing common hazards at home and school.
- Understanding safety signs.
- Identifying trusted adults and emergency contacts.
- Practicing what to say when asking for help.
- Planning a safe meeting point in a hypothetical scenario.

Boundaries:
- No live fire, frightening simulations, dangerous equipment, or unsupervised emergency drills.
- Children are taught to seek adult help, not to replace trained responders.

### 4. Maker Skills
**Focus:** Design, construction, testing, and iteration.

Examples:
- Building simple models from safe reusable materials.
- Sketching before making.
- Testing stability and usefulness.
- Recording feedback and improving a prototype.

Evidence:
- Describes a design decision.
- Tests against a simple criterion.
- Makes and explains an improvement.

### 5. Thinking Skills
**Focus:** Logic, analysis, evidence, and decisions.

Examples:
- Patterns and classification.
- Distinguishing fact from opinion.
- Comparing options against criteria.
- Breaking problems into smaller parts.
- Explaining a reasoned decision.

Evidence:
- States the problem clearly.
- Generates alternatives.
- Supports a choice with reasons.

### 6. Future Skills
**Focus:** Digital literacy, coding concepts, and responsible technology awareness.

Examples:
- Sequences, conditions, and repetition using beginner-friendly tools such as Scratch.
- Understanding that AI systems can produce errors.
- Classifying simple information.
- Recognizing privacy and safe online behavior.

Boundaries:
- No unnecessary personal accounts for children.
- Never upload a child's identifiable image, voice, or personal information to third-party AI services without a lawful basis, proper safeguards, and required parent permissions.
- Adult-supervised tools only.

### 7. Talent Discovery
**Focus:** Repeated exploration, observation, and opportunities to develop interests.

Explore areas such as:
- Scientific and engineering.
- Artistic and creative.
- Leadership and social.
- Analytical and logical.
- Language and expression.
- Physical and movement-based.

Observe:
- Interest and voluntary engagement.
- Initiative.
- Persistence.
- Creativity.
- Learning from mistakes.
- Progress over repeated opportunities.
- Desire to continue.

The process is **Explore → Observe → Develop → Showcase**. Never label a child as talented/not talented based on one test or activity. This is not psychological diagnosis or an intelligence test.

### 8. Literacy & Communication
**Focus:** Reading comprehension, writing, listening, discussion, and presentation.

Reading:
- Main idea and supporting details.
- Vocabulary in context.
- Fact versus opinion.
- Summarizing and asking questions.
- Checking whether information is supported.

Writing:
- Sentences and paragraphs.
- Stories, notes, instructions, and reports.
- Describing a design or project.
- Reflection and evidence logs.

Communication:
- Listening and turn-taking.
- Expressing opinions respectfully.
- Explaining decisions.
- Presenting work to an audience.
- Responding to questions.

Literacy is embedded in the other seven dimensions through instructions, planning, logs, research, collaboration, and presentations.

## Learning cycle

1. **LEARN:** Introduce a concept through a short explanation, story, demonstration, or example.
2. **PRACTICE:** Let children try the skill with scaffolding.
3. **SOLVE:** Apply it to a meaningful challenge.
4. **CREATE:** Produce a model, written artifact, plan, presentation, or digital project.
5. **REFLECT:** Identify what worked, what was difficult, and what to improve.

## Mastery rubric

| Level | Description |
|---|---|
| 1 – Needs support | Needs continuous guidance to perform the skill |
| 2 – Guided | Performs the skill with prompts or partial assistance |
| 3 – Independent | Performs the skill independently in a familiar context |
| 4 – Transfer | Applies the skill in a new or changed context |
| 5 – Innovate & explain | Improves the approach and can explain the reasoning |

Assess observable behavior and artifacts. Compare each child primarily with their own previous performance. Do not use the rubric as a fixed label or diagnosis.

## Child Development Portfolio

Each child may have a portfolio containing:
- Learning goals and baseline.
- Skill observations and assessment evidence.
- Project artifacts and reflections.
- Reading and writing samples.
- Talent exploration notes.
- Trainer feedback and next-step suggestions.
- Home activities and parent feedback.
- Photos or recordings only with separate, informed parent permission.

Keep only necessary data, restrict access, and define retention and deletion rules.

## Implementation quality

Every activity should specify:
- Target dimension(s).
- Observable learning objective.
- Age suitability.
- Materials and preparation.
- Facilitator steps.
- Adaptations for different learning needs.
- Safety controls.
- Evidence to collect.
- Reflection prompt.
